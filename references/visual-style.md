# Visual style guide (from frame analysis of the reference "Why You'd Never Want To Meet A Sperm Whale")

Reference: 9:59, 1280x720, 30 fps. Only 38 hard cuts in 10 minutes: it is a continuous animated world, not a slideshow.
Frames studied: every 2 s (300 frames) + 4 fps windows at 1:10, 4:08, 5:12.

## 1. The 10 rules that separate it from a slideshow

1. **Scenes persist and build.** One scene lives 6-20 s and covers 2-6 sentences. New sentences ADD to it (an object slides in, a label appears, a counter fills) instead of replacing it. Example 1:04-1:18: whale holds, "~16 M" bar draws, bus drives in at 1:12, bus fades as 7 elephants pop in one by one at 1:16, then a push-in to "SPERM WHALE".
2. **Illustrations, not icons.** Every subject is a custom flat illustration with 3-4 tones of shading, texture and a soft shadow: whale with skin wrinkles and scars, diver with tank, regulator, mask glint, fins; ships with sails and rigging; squid with suckers. Proportions are correct and characters are big (40-70% of the frame).
3. **One palette.** Desaturated navy/teal water (#0B1D26 to #1F4A5C), grey-beige animals, ONE warm accent (mustard fins), red (#E04545) only for threat text/stamps, green only for "safe/scan" ticks and radar. Sepia/paper for history and documents.
4. **Text is small and placed in the world.** 16-28 px at 720p (about 2-3% of frame height), uppercase bold geometric sans, white. Placed top-center, under the object, or next to what it names. Threat lines red with a thin red underline. Max 1-2 text items on screen. Never big pills stacked in a corner.
5. **The camera acts.** Macro close-up of the diver's gear (0:14-0:22), slow push into the jaw to show teeth (1:26-1:40), tilt down following a dive with a scrolling depth ruler (2:42-2:50), top-down spotlight cone (6:02-6:08), x-ray scan line sweeping across the head (4:12).
6. **Data is drawn inside the scene.** Measure line under the animal, dB meter that fills green to red (3:24-3:36), pie chart (7:14), speed bars (6:14-6:18), "volume contest" LED meters (6:54), icon arrays where 1 icon = 10,000 whales (8:02-8:10), carbon grid squares (9:12-9:20), radar screen in green (3:18, 6:20), stomach that fills with beaks while a counter runs (2:28-2:32).
7. **Comedy props.** Stick-figure scientists at a whiteboard (3:58-4:04), stick man in a dark hallway with eyes appearing (6:22-6:24), "SPERM WHALE DINER" receipt (2:34), "OFFICIAL COMPLAINT" form (5:58), book cover (5:28), "TRANSLATING WHALE..." progress dialog + CANCEL stamp (4:54), rubber stamps (STOPPED, MONSTER, COMPLAINT, WINNER).
8. **Impact effects.** Ram at 5:17: white flash, shockwave ring expanding, debris particles, short shake, then a red label. Reveals use a quick blur-in; scene changes use a 6-10 frame blur dissolve.
9. **Real footage as punctuation.** 5-6 stock clips of 3-6 s at emotional peaks: 0:24 freediver beside a sperm whale, 0:36-0:38 humpback breach, 0:40 orcas, 2:16 freediver, 8:30-8:34 aerial whale. Each is graded darker/cooler to match the palette.
10. **The "you" character recurs** in the same design throughout (0:00, 0:58, 2:52 as a pool swimmer with a 0:14 breath timer, 3:44 scanned in green, 6:14, 6:40, 9:30), always tiny next to the whale for scale.

## 3. Pacing numbers
- About 60 scenes in 10 min, so a scene averages 10 s; inside a scene a new element arrives every 2-3 s.
- Text on screen at any moment: 0-2 items, mostly 2-5 words.
- Stock footage: about 6 clips, about 25 s total (4%), all at emotional peaks.
- Camera never fully static: drift 1-3% scale or 10-40 px pan over a scene, plus subject motion.

## 4. Lessons from user review of blue whale v2
- The "you" diver must read as one body: head joined to the torso by a neck/shoulder; fins in a high-visibility colour (set in brand.json character_colors.fins, e.g. orange or lime) so the character is spotted instantly even when tiny.
- Every body part must be readable at a glance (user review v3): two separate legs with knees kicking out of phase (near leg lighter, far leg darker), torso with BCD vest + weight belt, neck, round hooded head, a framed mask (dark frame, light glass, glare), regulator with hose to the tank, two arms with elbows and a gloved hand holding a torch. Thin dark outlines between parts. Never a single fused silhouette.
- The first 5-10 s need menace: something big moving in the haze far away (a silhouette, a shark crossing) while the rule is stated.
- The hero animal must feel enormous and alive on its first appearance: show its signature action at full scale (blue whale = front-view lunge charging at camera, mouth wide, baleen, krill pouring in), bleed it off the frame, and keep the tiny diver in shot for scale.
- Side-view whales: flukes are horizontal, so they read as a thin blade from the side; a vertical forked tail makes a whale look like a fish.
- Lunge pose: lower jaw drops ~25 deg, pleated throat balloons back to the belly, baleen fringe hangs from the upper jaw, dark maw between.

## 5. What our first render (blue whale v1) did wrong
- New unrelated card every 3 s (194 beats = 194 scenes) instead of about 60 persistent scenes.
- Clip-art icons (emoji-like bus, elephant) instead of shaded illustrations.
- Big label pills stacked bottom-left on every beat; too much text.
- Labels repeated the narration word for word.
- Few in-scene diagrams; no props, no impact FX, no mode switches (x-ray, radar).
- Only 2 stock clips.


## Lessons from entry 02 (giant squid) QA
- Silhouettes on night water vanish if they are pure black on near-black. Give every silhouette a dark fill plus a cold rim light (stroke ~rgba(150,210,225,0.5), 2-3 px) and, for the hero, a faint glowing eye. Check the first 4 s frame for a visible menace shape.
- Check facing direction against the target: an animal whose arms/mouth point left must approach a target that sits to its LEFT (the lure scene first rendered the squid touching the lure with its tail).
- Bars labels are a fixed 260 px column: keep them to 1-2 short words (GIANT SQUID, YOU, USUAL YEAR) and put numbers in a Tag at the end of each bar.
- Legend/engraving monsters: tentacles must rise from the sea, arch over the hull and curl onto the deck; straight vertical strokes under a ship read as a pier.
- A Balance with a long animal on one pan looks broken; use "animal = N people" side by side instead.
- Measure lines near the bottom edge: keep y <= 990 so the label under the line is not cut off.


## Lessons from entry 02 review (opening scored 3-4/10)
- Make every video's motion graphics its own: design scenes around this animal's signature (squid: tentacle grab, ink, spark trail, top-down rise), never reuse the last video's layouts as a template.
- The hero must look GIANT: vivid painted colors (mottled skin, pale undersides, sucker rows), long dramatic limbs, and constant scale cues - a wall of skin sliding past the diver, an eye bigger than the diver's head, camera pans along a body that keeps going, the diver tiny beside it.
- Opening shots: something enormous passes in the first 2-4 s, the grab is a whip + flash + shake + camera punch-in with layered SFX (whip, wet grab, braam, panic bubbles), then a depth cut-away that drops the camera through the water column.
- Use real maps for every place in the script: lib/maps.tsx Globe fly-in (spin, zoom, pin drop + card) and FlatWorld range band. Needs `npm i d3-geo topojson-client world-atlas` (+ @types/d3-geo @types/topojson-client). Credit "Natural Earth (public domain)".
- Generate scene-specific SFX with ElevenLabs (tools/sfx.py LIB) instead of reusing generic ones; normalize to -20 LUFS.
- Music: if the user supplies tracks, assign them per chapter by mood, crossfade 2 s, normalize, sidechain-duck under the voice, and keep the bed about 20 dB under the voice (around -33 LUFS integrated in the final mix).
- When real images would help (archive photos, old engravings), send the user Wikimedia Commons public-domain links to download; check each file page's licence.


## Lessons from entries 03-04 (humpback, basking shark)
- STORY: every animal gets its own story engine and section order built from its central paradox (basking shark: shrink the viewer into its food). No default "What would you actually do?" quiz, no "Twist 1 / Twist 2" pair, no stock transitions, no title-phrase line, no dive-log circular close by default. Keep the brand opener line and the CTA.
- CURIOSITY LOOPS: do not cut questions - layer loops. Plant a specific mystery early ("hold on to that liver", "1,500 teeth it never eats with"), keep 2-3 open at once, pay each off later by name. Only drop the mechanical question at the end of every section. On screen, the LoopChips tracker shows "? N OPEN" and ticks each one when it is answered.
- ALWAYS stop for script approval before voicing or building, even if the user says to go end to end.
- Predators and the hero must look menacing or vivid (open jaws, teeth, dark lighting, lunging at camera). Text on light backgrounds must be dark, never yellow/white. Paper scenes: dim the paper so white labels read.
- Real footage placed after a scene's first frame MUST sit in its own Sequence (Clip helper), or it plays from the wrong point and freezes.
- Use the animal's real sounds when public domain (NPS, NOAA) and real photos (NOAA / Wikimedia public domain), credited on screen and in the description. Wikimedia rate-limits scripted downloads: fetch at most a couple of files, otherwise give the user links.
- Small fixes: render only the changed frame ranges (--frames=a-b --muted) and overlay them on the existing final with ffmpeg; amix new SFX onto the existing audio. Much faster than a full re-render.
- Thumbnail text must contain YOU (ideally red) and be specific to this video's story, not a phrase other channels use.
